How it works

  1. Load your subtitles. Drop an .srt or .vtt file, or paste the text. It is read on your device, never uploaded.
  2. Fix the timing. Shift by a fixed amount, sync two lines to their real times to fix drift, or convert between frame rates. The table shows old and new times live.
  3. Save the synced file. Download it in the same format with “synced” added to the name, or copy the text. Cue text, tags and styling stay exactly as they were.

Questions, answered

Are my subtitle files uploaded anywhere?

No. Subtitle Timing Shifter runs entirely in your browser. Your file is read into memory on your device, processed with JavaScript, and never sent to a server. You can disconnect from the internet and it keeps working.

The subtitles start in sync but drift later. How do I fix that?

A constant offset will not help with drift. Use Two-point sync: enter the real start time of an early line and of a late line, and every cue is stretched between them. If the drift comes from a frame rate mismatch, for example 25 fps subtitles on a 23.976 fps video, the Frame rate tab does the same with one click.

Will it change my text, styling or encoding?

Only the time codes change. Cue numbers, identifiers, italics and other tags, WebVTT headers, notes and position settings are kept as they were, along with the original line endings. Files in older encodings such as Windows-1252 or UTF-16 are saved as UTF-8, and the tool says when that happens.

Is it really free?

Yes. It is open source under the MIT license, with no account, no daily limits and no ads. If it saves you a paid subscription, you can say thanks on Ko-fi.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After your first visit the page is cached by a service worker, so it loads without a connection. You can also install it from your browser's menu as an app.
